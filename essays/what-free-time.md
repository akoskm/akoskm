# What Free Time?

*Draft — October 2026. Iterate before publishing.*

---

A couple of days ago, in our daily stand-up, the team was talking about what they do in their free time. Video games, doom scrolling, the usual. Then they asked me.

I said, "What do you mean? What is that word? Free... free what?"

We laughed. But the joke was true. Most of them don't have kids. I do. For me, free time isn't a thing you have — it's a thing you steal.

Then someone said, "Oh, you probably just go for a bike ride." And yeah, I do. But here's the thing: saying I don't have free time wasn't quite accurate either. I do have free time. I just don't experience it as free.

When a slot opens up — an hour, maybe two — I don't think about what to do next. My mind already knows. It's either a bike ride, a lifting session, working on my newsletter, or building one of the apps I made for my wife and for us at home. [LINK: Numi — breastfeeding tracker, numiapp.com] [LINK: SubSub — subscription tracker, subsub.app]

That's what I meant. I don't reach the phase where I could doom scroll, because my mind already knows what it can reach for. I'm occupied a hundred percent of the time — even though the things filling that time aren't work. They're the things that give my life meaning.

Outside of work and family, of course. It's not that workouts and side projects are the only things that fulfill me. They complete my character, alongside family and work.

Before we had kids, we were absolutely sure our free time would suffer. We knew life would feel completely different — and it was worth it. But we also assumed we'd have less time for the things we wanted to do.

Then something interesting happened. The year we had our kid was the year I published my first ebook. And that same year, three more. I launched a video course. I wrote my newsletter more consistently than I ever had before or since.

The year before, I was working part-time on purpose. I wanted the space to figure out what kind of business to build, what ideas were worth pursuing. Six months to a year of almost nothing but that. And I accomplished nothing.

Compare that to having a kid — and launching three ebooks, a video course, and a regular newsletter.

Having restricted free time, where my mind already knows what's next, turned out to be life-changing. It became an obligation to focus on the few things I could actually do and actually wanted to do. And because I didn't have much choice, I did them.

That's what having a kid caused. Not less time. A reminder that I have less time — so I'd better make the most of it, on the things that truly matter.
